## What is Git?

The best-known definition: **Git is a version control system**. In practice, here's what it does:

- **It tracks every file in your project**: every change, addition or deletion is recorded.
- **It keeps the history** of all those changes. Broke part of the project? You can **roll back** to the last stable version.
- **It knows who did what**: every change is tied to the person who made it.

## Why use it? A simple example

Picture a team working on the same project, each person on their own PC.

**Without Git**, everyone edits files on their side, then drops them into a shared folder. The result: people overwrite each other's files, changes get lost, and nobody knows which version is the right one.

**With Git**, everyone **pushes** their changes to an online platform such as **GitHub**, **GitLab** or **Bitbucket**. The whole team can then **pull** those changes, build on top of them and push their own, without overwriting anything.

It's a very simple example, but you'll explore all of this in detail in the next chapters.
